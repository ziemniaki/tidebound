# TIDEBOUND

## Game bible

**Version 1.28 | 24 September 2026**  
**Creative direction: Wojciech Krzyżanowski**  
**Working title: Tidebound | Full story spoilers**

A solitary Pokémon journey through a wounded coastal land, where the sea keeps the dead and love can become an excuse to refuse their absence.

This is the consolidated creative record for the whole game. It expands and supersedes the short design record dated 8 September 2026. It preserves established decisions, outlines a playable narrative, and identifies what still needs invention. It describes the intended game, not a claim that these chapters have already been built.

**Current playable milestone: 0.7.20, Natu's dark prelude before the two false bedrooms.** This revision implements the previously reserved lighthouse cellar/vault and the first dock-city museum hall. Earlier statements reserving those spaces describe the 0.6 necklace milestone and are superseded for this limited visit. The museum heist, the sabre's magical use and the final confrontation remain future content.

### How to read this document

**Confirmed** means explicitly established or accepted in the worldbuilding conversation. **Proposed** means a possible implementation, interpretation, or new connective detail. **Open** means a decision has not been made. **Intentional mystery** means the finished story should withhold an answer. **Prototype** describes existing content or temporary mechanics, which do not automatically become final lore.

These labels apply to the passage or subsection they introduce. In mixed passages, individual statements carry their own label. The proposed chapter order is an editorial outline built around confirmed events. A useful proposal is not canon merely because it appears here. Explicit later decisions from the creative director take precedence.

The game bible governs fictional facts, characterisation, emotional intent, and narrative relationships. The separate developer guide, `AGENTS.md`, governs implementation and continuity. Keep both current. Do not resolve a contradiction by silently changing established lore.

## 1. The emotional promise

### 1.1 The experience

**Confirmed.** The dominant feeling is dark, sad, nostalgic, and haunted: "bottomless sadness" interrupted by moments of absolute terror and significant beauty and hope. Japanese horror, including the user's association with *Ringu*, informs the presence of an ancient, majestic, frightening sea. *Dark Souls* informs solitude, wounded places, obscure histories, brief companionship, and encounters that retain emotional weight after they end.

The game remains recognisably a Pokémon adventure. The player travels with individual creatures, learns their habits, battles, catches, explores, and gradually understands a region. The familiar structure acquires a different meaning when companions can disappear and many human institutions have fallen silent.

**Design interpretation.** The central emotional movement is attachment followed by reinterpretation. A frightening apparition becomes a friend. A warm friend returns and becomes a horror. A familiar sanctuary becomes toxic. A supposedly fortunate gift becomes evidence of a mother's hidden ritual. The same place or relationship should mean something different after the player learns more.

### 1.2 Solitude and its exceptions

**Confirmed.** There is no conventional rival or professor. The player is fundamentally alone. Trainer battles are relatively sparse. Riddles, sad conversations, exploration, and helping people carry much of the journey. Some quests succeed; some fail despite sincere effort.

**Design interpretation.** Solitude needs exceptions to remain perceptible. Koga must be warm enough to miss. The mother's house must feel like a home before its mystery deepens. A resting fire should sometimes offer uncomplicated relief. Pokémon should have small recognisable behaviours outside their usefulness in battle.

Hope should produce observable changes. A repaired roof, a person eating again, a path made safe, or a companion returning can matter without repairing the entire world. Do not make every kindness a trap or every survivor secretly monstrous. Constant betrayal would weaken the special force of pseudo-Koga.

### 1.3 The recurring question

**Design interpretation.** How far can someone go to protect what they love before protection becomes possession, confinement, or destruction?

The villagers want Koga back. Pseudo-Koga protects a settlement by poisoning it. Mother alters her child's relation to death. The player tries to retrieve fallen companions. The ancient wrong concerns greed and its victims. These stories should illuminate one another without becoming mechanically identical or morally interchangeable.

The game should allow grief, loyalty, and the desire to save others to remain valuable. Their vulnerability to corruption does not make affection itself a mistake.

## 2. Canon at a glance

The following are confirmed foundations. Later sections distinguish their unresolved details.

- The region has an entirely new map, story, and custom regional Pokédex, including regional variants and a new evolution beyond Xatu.
- The sea herself is a majestic dark entity. Her deepest nature remains partly beyond explanation.
- The protagonist lives in a lighthouse with their elderly mother, its sole keeper.
- Mother believes in the water entities' mythology. Her ritual was not performed solely for the child's benefit and keeps the protagonist forever a child. Its full religious purpose remains open.
- The starting village's other permanent human residents are all elderly. Casual comments about the protagonist's unchanging youth foreshadow the truth.
- Mother's guilt made her exceptionally careful and overprotective. Her own advancing age now forces her to release the child from this gentle containment and prepare them for the world.
- The early account is domestic: Mother says the protagonist is old enough to explore and that she wants to teach them while she still has time. The ritual and arrested childhood are late revelations.
- The setting evokes the past: no advanced electronic technology. Books, runes and magic fit the world. A precise historical period is not fixed; steampunk remains a future possibility.
- The opening proceeds from the two false bedroom puzzles and bedroom play with Natu, through Mother's talk and Pookie's first walk, to a household companion choice. The oil and lamp errand follows. The chosen trio is Natu, Makuhita and Poochyena.
- No professor, conventional rival, or normal gym circuit structures the adventure.
- Criminals prey on deserted, impoverished communities. Dangerous wild Pokémon also genuinely torment people.
- The criminal organisation's desperate, foolish actions unleash water demons and intensify the later crisis.
- Shrines of old masters replace gyms. Most later shrines stand empty until special incense summons a master for a battle.
- Dive is required for the intended world and late exploration.
- A substantial forest occupies the region; its central placement is the leading proposal.
- Natu fills the common-bird role. A special item eventually enables a powerful regional evolution after Xatu.
- Regional Lapras is Water/Ghost: frightening apparition, beloved companion, unsettling revelation, then a late connection to a Water/Ghost legendary's magic.
- Young Koga is a lively, ambitious, warm prodigy. He discovers Poison Pokémon's usefulness against demonic enemies.
- Koga and his absent mother inspired a winter mountain dojo community. His sister is a major character and a trainer the player can battle.
- Ninja Apprentices concentrate in this part of the map and respectfully invite the player to train with them.
- Rumours associate prolonged exposure to poisonous Pokémon with corruption of the mind.
- Real Koga helps for a time, disappears, and remains missing when his storyline ends.
- Desperate villagers conjure a poison imitation of Koga. It contains no real Koga and is not his returned soul.
- The false reunion initially feels deeply comforting, including companionship in the astral plane. Cruelty and extreme corruption follow.
- Pseudo-Koga poisons the white winter settlement to defend it, producing purple contamination and green sludge.
- Before fighting him, the player confronts the poisoning of a cherished forest refuge and a corrupted Suicune.
- Suicune can be killed or captured. Healing him yields a Water/Grass form. A unique mystic sabre enables Behemoth Blade, the intended solution to a final water-demon boss.
- The game remains finishable without Suicune, but that victory leads to a darker ending with harm the player cannot repair.
- Ordinary poison cures, Full Heals, and normal Pokémon Centre recovery do not belong in the intended game loop.
- Timed fire recovery and journeys into a foggy astral plane replace the familiar recovery structure. Fallen companions must be found, fought, and caught again, with a severely limited opportunity.
- A late magical cemetery preserves fallen companions. Its shrine master can fight with the player's actual lost Pokémon and has his own impressive team when needed.
- The final shrine concerns facing oneself and discovering one's own capacity for corruption.
- Mother imbued an existing human protagonist with water magic or a curse. This enables astral traversal and makes the protagonist partly akin to the demons, which cling to them as a vessel.
- The demons were once peaceful inhabitants of the land. Human greed, ancient and present, produced their suffering and the present haunting.
- The story makes the demons' grievance convincing. The protagonist nevertheless chooses to protect people because innocents suffer and human kinship remains stronger.

## 3. The sea, magic, death, and personhood

### 3.1 The sea herself

**Confirmed.** The sea is a majestic dark presence, treated as an entity rather than scenery. Her beauty, scale, and terror belong together. Learning the history of particular demons must not reduce her to a completely explained machine or a single villain with a comprehensible agenda.

**Open.** Her precise relation to the Water/Ghost legendary: manifestation, inhabitant, emissary, vessel, or something else. The legendary and the final water-demon boss have not been established as the same creature. Do not merge them by default.

**Proposed presentation.** The sea can appear unusually still when it should be rough. A wave can arrive without the expected preceding sound. A light can remain below the water after the lighthouse beam passes. Use such events sparingly and without an immediate explanation. The largest terrors need not fit into a sprite.

### 3.2 The water demons

**Confirmed.** Their origin lies in formerly peaceful inhabitants harmed through human greed. Ancient actions and contemporary actions belong to the same causal history. Their grievance is real; their present violence also reaches people who did not commit the original wrong.

**Open.** Whether all these former inhabitants were human, Pokémon, or a community including both. "Once peaceful inhabitants" must not silently become "every demon is a drowned human." The number of entities, their individual identities, and the process that transformed them remain to be designed.

**Design interpretation.** "Demon" can be the living population's name for a dangerous being, not an omniscient judgement about its original nature. Distinguish water-demon corruption from ordinary Ghost Pokémon and ordinary Water Pokémon. Lapras's Ghost typing alone does not make it malevolent.

### 3.3 The protagonist's condition

**Confirmed.** The protagonist existed as a person before Mother imbued them with water magic. The curse explains their ability to traverse the astral plane and return. At first, this ability appears to be a fortunate coincidence. Much later, the mother's intervention becomes known. Their affinity makes them an available vessel for the demons and explains why they occupy a special position in the final conflict.

**Confirmed addition.** The ritual keeps the protagonist forever a child. Mother is a believer in the water entities' mythology and did not perform it strictly for the child's sake. Her guilt contributed to years of overprotective care and gentle containment. Her own advanced age now makes continued containment impossible, so she begins preparing the protagonist to venture out.

**Open.** The ritual's exact religious purpose, date, circumstances, price, Mother's full knowledge, and whether she intended the final confrontation. Saving a dying child remains an unconfirmed earlier idea and cannot serve as a complete account of her motive. The protagonist's chronological age, subjective experience of time, and whether childhood arrest affects psychological development need deliberate decisions. Arrested ageing does not imply invulnerability or contradict the established death mechanics. Do not turn the already existing child into a magically manufactured person.

**Proposed dramatic emphasis.** The revelation should alter the meaning of care received at home. It need not erase that care. Love and a violation of the child's autonomy can coexist, but the story must establish the actual circumstances before judging them.

### 3.4 Pseudo-Koga's different origin

**Confirmed.** The imitation is a conjured construction of poison magic. No existing Koga-person was transformed into it. Real Koga's whereabouts remain unknown. Its apparent familiarity initially encourages the protagonist to imagine a shared condition, making the eventual distinction painful.

**Continuity rule.** Participation in the astral plane cannot prove that a figure contains the soul of the person it resembles. The shared quest is an experience of apparent companionship, not reliable evidence that Koga has returned. Explain enough about the villagers' act to settle the imitation's origin without answering every metaphysical question about magical behaviour.

The required distinction concerns origin and identity. Do not soften the reveal with a last-minute fragment of real Koga secretly surviving inside the clone. The player's grief over the reunion remains valid even though that reunion was false.

### 3.5 Poison as defence and contamination

**Confirmed.** Koga discovers that Poison Pokémon help against demonic enemies. The winter town's later pollution visibly demonstrates a real defensive effect. Rumours claim prolonged exposure can poison the mind.

**Open.** The specific battle effect, environmental mechanism, and truth of the mental-corruption rumour. The rumour does not establish the cause of real Koga's disappearance. Pseudo-Koga's deterioration does not retrospectively prove that ordinary Poison Pokémon make every trainer cruel.

**Proposed distinction.** Careful handling of a dangerous defensive substance contrasts with uncontrolled saturation. Koga's skill can involve restraint, observation, and care for his Pokémon. The imitation can turn one useful discovery into an absolute rule: more poison means more safety. This supports the theme while preserving the real Koga's warmth.

### 3.6 The astral plane

**Confirmed.** A foggy soul world where the protagonist can appear after death and search for fallen companions. These companions roam and must be fought and caught again. The realm also supports a significant shared quest with pseudo-Koga.

**Proposed geography.** Astral spaces echo living places without matching them perfectly. A familiar path may reach a shore that does not exist above. A home may have the shape of a remembered doorway but no interior. Repeated motifs establish a relationship between the worlds without requiring a complete cosmological diagram.

**Open.** Whether every dead being enters this plane, what disappearance beyond recovery means, how time behaves there, and whether the sea contains or merely touches it. Do not make speculative answers necessary to understand ordinary quest objectives.

## 4. History and the present crisis

### 4.1 Historical anchors

**Confirmed sequence, with dates open.** Peaceful inhabitants once lived in the land. Ancient human greed harmed them and contributed to their transformation into haunting entities. Later generations inherited a damaged world. Present exploitation renews or worsens the older injury. The criminal organisation eventually unleashes water demons through desperation and recklessness.

Shrines, old masters, the mother's magic, and the legendary belong to the region's larger mystery. Their exact historical relationships are not yet fixed. A legend spoken by a villager is not automatically an objective account.

### 4.2 Proposed historical outline

**The inhabited coast.** The places later found underwater were once places of ordinary life. Proposed environmental evidence includes household thresholds, shared paths, worked stone, and objects showing relations between people and Pokémon. Their former inhabitants should become imaginable before their suffering becomes exposition.

**The ancient taking.** A powerful human group extracted something or appropriated a place at others' expense. A drowned settlement, broken compact, or sacrificial protection scheme are possible explanations. None has been selected. The eventual choice should make material self-interest legible and connect to what the present criminals want.

**The age of keeping.** Proposed: later communities developed practices that contained danger without fully repairing the wrong. Lamps, bells, offerings, incense, and maintained thresholds could survive as habits. This would explain why neglected everyday duties matter. It does not establish that every shrine master participated in the ancient crime.

**The thinning settlements.** Present poverty, abandoned buildings, unreliable travel, and diminished protection create opportunities for predation. Exact causes of depopulation remain open; avoid attributing every missing resident to one supernatural event.

**The present breach.** Criminal interference crosses a threshold that local practices had kept partially closed. The act must be concrete enough to investigate and stop repeating. It should arise from the organisation's established exploitation, not from an unrelated villain appearing late.

### 4.3 Proposed causal chain

The following is a proposed connection between confirmed arcs:

1. Criminal control of supplies and routes makes isolated settlements more vulnerable.
2. Sacred objects or submerged valuables become profitable targets. Early disturbances expose people to limited demonic danger.
3. Koga's poison defence offers a practical local answer, and communities increasingly depend on him.
4. Koga disappears for reasons the game never establishes. The crisis continues without him.
5. Desperate villagers attempt the poison conjuration. Its apparent success encourages dependence on the imitation.
6. Pseudo-Koga expands the defence until the winter town becomes toxic. Water carries contamination into the cherished forest.
7. The player confronts Suicune's corruption, then the false protector. Exposing the conjuration resolves that deception only.
8. The larger criminal breach and its consequences drive the descent toward the ancient coast and the final demons.

The timing of the major breach can move. The fixed requirement is that Koga's defence, the villagers' desperation, organised predation, and the later water crisis belong to one connected world. Criminals do not have to explain Koga's disappearance to connect these arcs.

## 5. Region, geography, and settlements

### 5.1 Regional structure

**Confirmed.** A new region includes a coastal starting town, a large forest, a snowy mountain dojo settlement, at least one other town central to the Koga storyline, underwater exploration, shrines, and an astral realm.

**Proposed organising geography.** A watershed connects snowy heights to streams, forest, river settlement, estuary, and sea. The great forest occupies much of the middle of the map. This makes travel and later contamination comprehensible. The mountain is visible from some lowland clearings; the lighthouse remains a distant reference from parts of the coast.

The region should fold back through earlier places as abilities and knowledge change. Exact compass positions, distances, town count, map scale, route names, and regional name remain open. Do not interpret the following list as a finished overworld map.

### 5.2 Shiohama: the starting town

**Confirmed role; working name.** A poor, partly deserted coastal town, close enough to the lighthouse that Mother and child have ordinary obligations to its remaining residents. It establishes deprivation, the sea's dominance, and the small scale of early help. Its other permanent human inhabitants are all old; the eternally young protagonist is the exception. The reason for the village's age distribution remains open. Do not infer that every resident participated in the ritual.

**Prototype.** The opening includes the keeper's house, lantern room, coastal village, oil seller, pier apparition, and route toward the forest. The oil errand teaches relationships and geography before a heroic identity develops.

**Proposed town character.** Salt has whitened wood and stone. Some shutters remain closed even during business hours. A few residents maintain places whose former purpose exceeds the remaining population. An abandoned rail halt is an earlier proposal, not a required feature or confirmed regional technology choice.

Potential residents include someone who sells lamp oil, a person waiting for a boat that no longer calls, and a neighbour who remembers Mother before the protagonist does. These are roles for development, not final named characters.

**Confirmed opening revision, implemented in prototype 0.4.0.** The player begins in their bedroom playing with Wick the Natu. Mother enters and asks them to come to the main hall. Makuhita is visibly moving boxes while she talks about letting the child explore alone; she pauses to scold his sloppiness sweetly. She suggests taking Pookie the Poochyena for a walk.

Pookie sleeps outside beside the lighthouse. She follows the player for a walk of 100 actual steps around the village. Only if they approach the pier does she bark at an unseen presence and run to its end. Speaking to her there restores following. This implies that she senses Lapras, without showing the player a creature. The detour is optional: the walk can finish without going near the pier. Returning home together after 100 steps completes it; returning without Pookie does not.

Mother, Natu and Makuhita are at home when the pair returns. Mother explains that the world is unsafe and permits the player to choose one of their three familiar companions. Only after selection does she ask for lamp oil. The seller stands beside his locked shop: he has lost its keys again in the northern forest. The player finds them among white flowers as an actual Key Item and returns them. He unlocks the door and goes inside; the player must enter to obtain the oil. Bringing it to Mother and tending the great lamp continues the domestic lesson. This order supersedes the earlier post-lamp companion choice.

**Confirmed foreshadowing direction.** Elderly residents make apparently affectionate comments about the protagonist's unchanged youth, such as "You're always looking young as ever!" Exact wording remains editable. A few such remarks should acquire their full meaning only later. Which residents know the truth, and how much they know, remain open.

**Narrative uses.** Establish home, display criminal pressure through ordinary needs, stage Lapras's first frightening encounter, and provide a return location where the same familiar sea later looks different. The town should not begin with a professor's laboratory.

**Confirmed expansion, prototype 0.6.0.** Recovering the keys also earns a homemade pie on a borrowed ceramic plate. Mother and child share it at home. Returning the plate leads into the robbery and a southward pursuit, described in 10.7. Two visiting youths from the surrounding coast commit the theft; they are not additional young permanent residents of Shiohama. The familiar household and village remain the emotional centre.

### 5.3 The lighthouse and headland

**Confirmed.** This is the protagonist's home. Mother, an old woman, is its sole keeper. Earlier concept art showing a small garden house does not override the later lighthouse-home decision.

**Working arrangement.** The keeper's domestic rooms and lantern room belong to one lighthouse home on a separate headland. A safe, legible coastal approach gives leaving and returning emotional weight. Temporary separate map interiors are a game representation of that home.

**Prototype and earlier proposal.** A second lamp points down into the water. Its ultimate purpose remains open. It may eventually relate to keeping, recognition, or the curse, but none of those explanations is confirmed.

Proposed domestic details include drying cloth, an old maintenance routine, an unfinished meal, and a place where the companion habitually waits. Establish Mother's age through everyday action and accumulated knowledge, not only through cryptic speeches.

**Confirmed vault seed.** The lighthouse contains an unusually large secure vault. The protagonist first hears of it when the oil seller considers where to keep the recovered necklace and casually suggests Ellie's enormous vault. The vault's location within the building, purpose, construction, contents and connection to the lower lamp remain open. This milestone adds only the remark. No vault room, access puzzle or explanatory scene is implemented.

### 5.4 The Listening Wood: the great forest

**Confirmed large forest; working name and central placement.** Natu is common here. The forest contains riddles, routes, old places, and a cherished protected refuge. It must be substantial enough to remain important beyond one introductory route.

**Prototype.** Only an opening section exists, with a traveller's resting fire, a Natu encounter, a dangerous pool, and a northern endpoint. This is not the entire intended forest.

**Proposed internal districts.** The old road connects inhabited places; a rain village survives under the canopy; a bell grove retains an old ritual; a white clearing holds the protected refuge; deeper paths approach mountain watercourses. These earlier working ideas can be combined or reduced when the actual map develops.

Before pollution, give the refuge at least one meaningful visit: a quiet task, a conversation, an indirect glimpse of Suicune, or a remembered sound. After pollution, retain enough geometry and detail for recognition. The player should know what changed.

Natu's familiar abundance offers a useful measure of disturbance. Proposed: the refuge's first warning is the absence of its ordinary calls, before the player sees sludge. This is an atmospheric option, not a fixed species behaviour rule.

### 5.5 Frostbell: the winter dojo settlement

**Confirmed role; working name.** A mountain settlement of honourable warriors, with a dojo or monastic training tradition inspired by the user's Shaolin-town idea. Koga and his mother inspired the community's project. His mother is absent; his sister relates its history.

**Proposed layout.** Snowy courtyards, training areas, shared kitchens, sheltered paths, gardens, and a place associated with Koga's mother. Daily work should make the settlement feel inhabited and disciplined. Its inhabitants can be warm even when the landscape is austere.

Ninja Apprentices invite respectful practice. Their signature tone is: "You look honourable. Help me grow as a warrior!" They are not a route full of hostile guards. An earlier proposal allows the player to decline ordinary practice battles. Their concentration makes this area an exception to the game's generally sparse trainer density.

**Confirmed transformation.** Pseudo-Koga poisons the settlement to defend it. The white town becomes purple with toxins and green with sludge. The defence must have a perceptible benefit against demons, so initial acceptance has a reason. The resulting sickness and environmental harm make that protection intolerable.

**Proposed aftermath.** Survivors clear one path or restore a shared room. The town need not instantly return to pristine white after the boss. Its altered state can register both damage and the beginning of repair.

### 5.6 Reed Hollow: the second Koga settlement

**Confirmed need for a second town; working name and river geography.** This community gives the Koga story a social world beyond the dojo. The leading outline places the villagers responsible for the conjuration here, although the exact participants and ritual site remain open.

**Proposed character.** A lowland river settlement depends on threatened routes and outside defenders. Reeds, damp workshops, moored boats, and patched communal buildings distinguish it from Frostbell. Residents know Koga through practical help, rather than primarily as a teacher or brother.

The village should contain different responses to his absence: someone searches, someone organises ordinary defence, someone demands a supernatural solution, and someone doubts it but stays silent. Avoid making "the villagers" a single mind with identical knowledge and guilt.

This is a strong location for contradictory return rumours, traces of preparation, and the eventual testimony that establishes the clone's origin. Those revelations should not also reveal where real Koga went.

### 5.7 The cherished refuge

**Confirmed.** A treasured, protected part of the forest becomes badly poisoned. An old guardian is dying. A rare, beautiful Pokémon has been corrupted. The player must confront the creature before the pseudo-Koga boss.

**Open.** The guardian's species or human identity, name, duties, and ultimate fate. "Dying" describes the encounter, not an already approved ending to every attempt to help the guardian.

The refuge should represent more than a container for Suicune. Its local relationships, wildlife, water, and customs should make the loss legible even if the player never catches him.

### 5.8 Beyond the Shore: astral spaces

**Confirmed realm; prototype map name.** Fog, roaming lost companions, uncertain distances, and recognisable fragments of living geography. The first visit teaches the cost of death. Later visits can carry story, including the shared pseudo-Koga quest.

**Proposed expansion.** Create several compact astral spaces with distinct relationships to nearby living places. They should not all be colour-filtered duplicates. The late cemetery can provide a more stable point of contact with loss; its exact relation to the astral plane remains open.

### 5.9 The submerged land

**Confirmed need for Dive and late ancient revelations; proposed specific sites.** A drowned settlement, submerged shrine approaches, and remains of ordinary habitation make the demons' former life tangible. The precise catastrophe that placed them underwater remains open.

Proposed exploration moves from navigable human-scale remains toward spaces that resist familiar interpretation. Environmental discoveries should establish historical evidence before the legendary account expands its meaning.

### 5.10 The final cemetery shrine

**Confirmed.** A very late shrine acts as a magical cemetery and a gallery of the player's fallen Pokémon. Its master tests the player's ability to face themselves, using lost companions when available.

**Open.** Name, location, architecture, relation to the water, and whether the gallery is reached from the living world, astral plane, or both. Do not place it permanently until the late traversal route is coherent.

### 5.11 City 2: the dock city

**Confirmed future canon; numbered working designation.** City 2 is an important, potentially principal dock city. Its identity centres on shipping, coastal trade, warehouses, salvage, and the movement of goods between isolated coastal settlements. Illegal resale and distribution operate alongside ordinary trade.

Team Abyss maintains a theft, fencing and illegal-distribution operation here. The small storehouse in the necklace quest belongs to this wider network. Early thieves mention buyers at the docks without explaining the city's full importance. Later exploration should make the economics of that network comprehensible.

**Confirmed future canon: the museum.** City 2 contains an important museum that becomes a major narrative location. It publicly displays the unique mystic sabre already connected to healed Suicune and the better final resolution. The museum's possession must eventually make historical sense, but its complete provenance remains undecided. Team Abyss later steals the sabre with incomplete understanding; see 7.5 and 10.6.

**Open.** Final city and museum names, exact map, population, curatorial details, full quest structure, heist timing and staging. The city and museum are not built in 0.6. The short southbound pursuit route leads in their direction without fixing a complete regional map.

### 5.12 City 4: a hometown mentioned over food

**Confirmed connection; numbered working designation.** City 4 is the oil seller's hometown and the source of the pie recipe. It first enters dialogue as an ordinary place someone comes from. The borrowed plate may later offer regional visual recognition there.

**Prototype choice.** The plate has two blue reeds painted on its rim. This is ordinary household pottery, not a magical object. Its recognisable design is available for later City 4 ceramics. City 4's final name, location, broader identity and quests remain open. Do not equate it with Frostbell or Reed Hollow merely because of its number or the painted reeds.

## 6. Character dossiers

### 6.1 The protagonist

**Confirmed.** Mother's child, raised in the lighthouse, apparently fortunate in their ability to return through the astral plane. Later they discover that their water curse makes them partly kin to the entities threatening the region. They ultimately protect human innocents despite recognising the demons' grievance.

**Confirmed.** The protagonist remains a child because of Mother's ritual. Their chronological age and awareness of the elapsed years remain open; do not infer a specific number of decades or a fixed adult mind in a child's body.

**Prototype.** Ren is a provisional default name, changeable through the opening journal. Exact apparent age, gender presentation, visual design, and final naming conventions remain open.

**Proposed arc.** A person begins with obligations close to home, becomes useful to strangers, mistakes usefulness for an obligation to save everything, and eventually accepts limits without abandoning care. Their relationship with pseudo-Koga offers an apparent escape from isolation: someone seems to understand being altered by magic. The cemetery tests whether accepting loss can coexist with continued action.

Do not make every player action morally wrong to force this arc. Their capacity for corruption is a question raised by their situation, not an accusation that ordinary exploration or unavoidable defeat proves wickedness.

### 6.2 Mother, the keeper

**Confirmed.** An elderly mother and the lighthouse's sole keeper. She believes in the water entities' mythology. She imbued the already existing protagonist with water magic for reasons not limited to the child's welfare. The ritual keeps the protagonist forever a child. Its nature and her complicity become clear only much later.

Her guilt made her exceptionally careful and overprotective. The familiar safety of home has also been gentle containment. She now recognises that she is very old and does not have long, so she must allow the protagonist to leave and learn to live beyond her care.

**Early presentation.** She says the child is old enough to explore the world and wants to teach them some things while she still can. After the first walk with Pookie, she permits one of the three house pets to accompany them. The oil and lamp lesson follows this choice. This should initially feel like an intimate family transition. Neither she nor the interface openly explains the ritual, guilt, or permanent childhood here.

**Working name: Ellie.** The protagonist still addresses her as Mother. The oil seller uses Ellie casually after the necklace is recovered.

**Open.** Final name, precise religious objective beyond the established mixed motive, ritual circumstances, relationship with the legendary, what she knows about the ancient wrong, and her final fate. She is not confirmed dead, sacrificed, secretly nonhuman, or the creator of the sea.

**Proposed character direction.** Practical affection, fatigue, old habits, and withheld knowledge. Let her speak plainly about oil, weather, and food. Occasional evasions then have more force. Her confession should answer specific questions about the protagonist rather than deliver the entire region's cosmology.

### 6.3 Koga, the real friend

**Confirmed.** Young, lively, warm, ambitious, and exceptionally talented. His discovery gives communities a useful defence. He helps the protagonist for a meaningful part of the journey before disappearing completely.

**Relationship requirement.** The player must know his actual Pokémon team well. Establish its members through several encounters and small behaviours. Final species and moves are open; record them as stable character facts once chosen. A brief single battle will not provide enough familiarity for the later absence to carry its intended weight.

**Proposed behaviour.** He enjoys progress, praises effort, talks to his own Pokémon naturally, and can admit that an experiment failed. His ambition should feel generous and alive. His eventual absence must not retroactively turn every earlier kindness into a clue that he was secretly corrupt.

**Intentional mystery.** His destination and fate. Do not provide a body, a final explanatory letter, an authenticated afterlife appearance, a hidden rescue, or an ending cameo that resolves the disappearance. Rumours may persist without becoming proof.

**Open continuity.** His relation to the familiar franchise character. Do not assume this is a canonical prequel or force later franchise events to explain his disappearance.

### 6.4 Koga's sister

**Confirmed.** A major character, a trainer the player can battle, and a source of stories about Koga and their absent mother. Her name, age, team, and final personal arc remain open.

**Proposed role.** She sustains the settlement while its charismatic prodigy travels. She knows his ordinary habits and his Pokémon, giving her a different basis for recognising discrepancies in the returned figure. She can want the reunion to be true without immediately trusting every aspect of it.

Her choices should influence the settlement: protecting apprentices, resisting escalating poison, investigating, or helping survivors. Do not make her only a source of exposition or a hostage. Do not automatically name her after an existing franchise character.

### 6.5 Koga's mother

**Confirmed.** She and Koga inspired the settlement's project. She is no longer there; his sister preserves the stories.

**Open.** Whether she died, left, vanished, or is absent for another reason. Her philosophy and what Koga understood or misunderstood about it need development. She and the protagonist's mother are different people; no relationship between them has been established.

### 6.6 Pseudo-Koga

**Confirmed.** The poison construction appears to restore the lost friend. It initially feels convincing and close, then acts out of character, becomes cruel and brutal, and deteriorates into a disfigured, cloudlike poison horror. Its final team is entirely different from real Koga's familiar team.

**Proposed dramatic progression.** It first provides useful help, then justifies a small disproportionate act as protection, then treats disagreement as danger, and finally attempts to make protection total. Let earlier affection be experienced fully before suspicion dominates.

The boss must resolve the immediate threat. Later evidence establishes the villagers' conjuration and confirms that this was never the returned real Koga. The player destroys the appearance of a dear friend, then learns that the original search has not advanced to an answer.

### 6.7 The villagers behind the conjuration

**Confirmed.** Desperate villagers attempt poison magic to bring back Koga and create the imitation instead.

**Proposed character distribution.** An organiser makes the decision; a participant supplies something necessary; a dissenter fails to stop the ritual; a resident believes the public account without knowing the method. These roles make responsibility specific without requiring a large cast.

Their actions should be understandable through what they fear and have already lost. Their desperation does not erase responsibility. Their later testimony should contain concrete evidence, not merely a rumour competing with all the others.

### 6.8 The old forest guardian

**Confirmed.** Dying amid the polluted refuge, the guardian identifies the corruption of its rare resident and gives the player a reason to act. Identity and final fate are open.

**Proposed direction.** The guardian remembers the place as a lived relationship rather than a treasure to possess. If human, give them ordinary duties and memories. If a Pokémon or spirit, establish how communication works before relying on a long explanation.

### 6.9 Travelling ninjas and apprentices

**Confirmed concept.** Travelling ninjas or comparable figures maintain small fires for limited recovery. Ninja Apprentices form a respectful trainer class near the mountain settlement.

**Proposed distinction.** Travellers connect scattered places through practical knowledge; apprentices seek growth through training. They need not all belong to one formal organisation. A recurring traveller can anchor trust without replacing the missing rival or accompanying the player continuously.

### 6.10 Criminal leaders and local people

**Open names and cast.** The organisation needs recognisable agents of predation, but Team Abyss is now the approved working name; no leader, executive roster or final uniform is approved. Local civilians need roles before biographies: a keeper, a seller, a boat operator, an apprentice, a bereaved neighbour, a caretaker.

**Proposed economy of character.** Revisit a modest cast as their circumstances change. A short altered line in a place the player remembers can carry more weight than introducing another tragic stranger at every turn.

### 6.11 The cemetery master

**Confirmed.** The final shrine leader presents a battle drawn from the player's fallen Pokémon when possible and otherwise uses an impressive team of his own. His trial concerns self-recognition and corruption.

**Open.** Identity, name, history, team, and relation to earlier masters. A gentle manner is an earlier proposal, not settled dialogue. The master need not accuse or explain everything for the encounter to be severe.

### 6.12 The oil seller

**Confirmed.** A sympathetic elderly neighbour in Shiohama, originally from City 4. Forgetful about his keys, overgenerous with food, mildly comic and sincerely grateful. His painful or ageing legs prevent him from chasing the two thieves. He remains polite even while distressed and notices the returned plate in the middle of the crisis.

The stolen pearl necklace belonged to his deceased wife. He reveals this only after its recovery, in restrained ordinary language. Her death has no fixed date. His wife, his own final name and their wider biography remain open.

**Emotional role.** He represents healthy attachment to someone who is gone. Keeping a beloved object and missing its wearer do not imply corruption. He wants the necklace back; he does not try to resurrect his wife or refuse the reality of her death. This provides a quiet counterpoint to later forms of destructive protection and conjuration.

He knows Mother as Ellie and knows the lighthouse has a very large vault. He also knows of the dock-city museum. His sources for those ordinary facts are not specified. Neither he nor the player understands the pearls' deeper significance. His knowledge is particular, never an excuse to deliver omniscient lore.

**Recurring narrative role.** Oil seller, home, southbound road, local theft, City 2's distribution, City 4's food and later magical ecology become connected through a neighbour's life. He does not knowingly introduce the final plot.

### 6.13 The early thieves

**Confirmed tone and role.** Two inexperienced young thieves from the surrounding coast have begun associating with Team Abyss. They are rude, opportunistic, bad liars and recognisably comic in the manner of early Pokémon antagonists. They know the lighthouse child locally but do not become a rival or traveling companion.

**Prototype names and rosters.** Toma uses a level-5 Rattata; Ivo uses a level-7 Poochyena. Bram, a petty storehouse runner, uses a level-6 Zigzagoon. These are adjustable implementation choices, not permanent character designs. A packer and lookout add several voices without requiring a battle against every criminal. Stock trainer art does not establish the organisation's final uniform.

## 7. Pokémon and the regional Pokédex

### 7.1 Dex principles

**Confirmed.** The game has a custom dex with regional variants and additional original development. Its size, generation coverage, complete species list, and availability curve remain open.

**Design interpretation.** Species should contribute to place, daily life, or a major relationship. Ordinary creatures must remain ordinary enough for exceptional ones to stand out. Not every Pokémon needs a horrific regional form. A familiar Natu or a companion's habitual movement can provide continuity within the darkness.

New forms require more than recolouring: narrative role, ecology, readable silhouette, battle function, and an acquisition route. Exact stats and learnsets belong in species specifications once approved, rather than being guessed here.

### 7.2 Regional Lapras

**Confirmed type and arc: Water/Ghost.**

**First encounter: fear.** The player encounters a ghost before understanding a companion. The event should feel wrong in a place that seemed familiar. The existing pier glimpse is a prototype of this beat, not the final sprite or complete encounter.

**Later: attachment.** The player learns to love Lapras. This requires repeated acts or experiences that establish character beyond a useful traversal ability. Proposed moments include waiting nearby, recognising the protagonist, or guiding them through a frightening passage. Exact recruitment and party status remain open.

**Then: the unsettling fact.** Something about Lapras becomes deeply disturbing after attachment forms. Its precise content is still open. New proposals include an involuntary link through which the sea recognises the protagonist or the burden of carrying traces of the drowned. These must remain proposals; no betrayal or concealed appetite is confirmed.

**Near the end: the legendary connection.** Lapras is revealed as imbued with the Water/Ghost legendary's magic. This should reinterpret earlier signs without cancelling the experiences that made it beloved.

**Current visual direction (0.7.8).** The user requested a direct edit of original Lapras: white and ghostly, recognisable silhouette, restrained mist and a distinctive head mark. The implemented draft uses a cold-cream body, mist-grey shell, jade-green eye and small faded-yellow forehead scar. This supersedes the earlier indigo-shell/ring-marking proposal. Preserve an expression capable of tenderness. The mark has no established lore explanation; do not invent one. See section 26 for implementation scope.

### 7.3 Natu, Xatu, and the regional evolution

**Confirmed.** Natu is the common regional bird, filling the everyday encounter role often held by Pidgey. A special item allows a powerful regional evolution after Xatu. This is a further evolution, not merely an alternative evolution replacing Xatu.

**Open.** Name, typing, ability, stats, appearance, item, and acquisition conditions. Dawn Glass and a traveller-like Psychic/Flying design are earlier working proposals. No final species data follows from those suggestions.

**Proposed thematic role.** An ordinary companion can develop into something suited to travelling between difficult places. This would let an early, familiar creature remain important without requiring a legendary. Its power must not quietly duplicate Suicune's unique hopeful-ending function.

**Prototype 0.5.1.** Wick, a level-7 Natu, first plays with the child in their bedroom and is one of three household companions available after Pookie's walk. The automatic starting Natu remains only in legacy saves. Names and levels are provisional. Natu remains the common regional bird regardless of the eventual starter decision; choosing a household individual need not make the species exclusive. Borrowed and fallback Natu in the astral mechanics remain separate gameplay accommodations.

### 7.3a The three house pets

**Confirmed structure.** These are already familiar household Pokémon, not strangers handed out by an external expert. Mother lets one accompany the protagonist after Pookie's walk, before the oil errand. The creative director prefers existing two-stage evolutionary lines so a custom third stage can be added. Three permanent final evolutions, rather than temporary transformations, are the design direction; custom third-stage designs remain open.

**User candidates.** Natu, the Meditite/Medicham line, and a possible Dark-type third companion. Meditite is the unevolved member of the Medicham line. Both it and Natu have Psychic typing, so choosing them together would create an intentional overlap.

**Selected for the playable opening.** Following the recommendation, the creative director authorized implementation. The 0.4.0 trio is Natu to Xatu (Psychic/Flying), Makuhita to Hariyama (Fighting), and Poochyena to Mightyena (Dark), with a custom third evolution planned for each line. The current nicknames are Wick, Maku and Pookie, all level 7; these names and balancing values are provisional. The suggested household roles are a quiet watchful bird, an affectionate helper with physical strength, and a loyal dog that guards the door. Possible final themes are a seer, a mountain guardian, and a supernatural watchdog. None of these themes fixes final typing, art, or species names.

Psychic, Fighting and Dark form an offensive advantage cycle, but not the symmetrical interaction of a simple starter triangle. Dark is immune to Psychic, and Natu's Flying typing changes the matchups further. Meditite's Psychic secondary type also changes its weaknesses. Balance should focus on the full journey and early move access, not merely three type labels. Each choice needs usable early answers to spectral enemies; a Fighting choice must not become helpless against Ghost Pokémon.

**Alternative proposal.** Keep Meditite in the trio if its meditative character matters more than distinct typings, or reserve it for the mountain community. Do not assign it to Koga's sister's team without a later decision.

### 7.4 Corrupted Suicune

**Confirmed creature and arc.** A very rare, beautiful Suicune in the cherished forest becomes thoroughly corrupted by poison. The player can kill him or catch him. Capture opens a healing responsibility.

**Working corrupted typing: Water/Poison.** The poison form was proposed during development; exact species implementation and battle properties remain open. The healed **Water/Grass** form is confirmed.

**Proposed presentation.** Retain beauty beneath damage. Familiar flowing forms can become heavy, stained, or painfully interrupted. The restored form should show recovery and new life rather than merely reversing a colour filter. Its association with grass and living water should be visible without cluttering the silhouette.

Healing must have an intelligible process. Its ingredients, places, duration, and possible intermediate behaviour are open. Do not make capture itself instantly cure the forest or everything downstream.

### 7.5 The mystic sabre and Behemoth Blade

**Confirmed.** Giving Suicune a unique mystic sabre enables Behemoth Blade. This is the intended way to destroy a final water-demon boss and achieve the more hopeful resolution.

**Open.** The sabre's final name, origin, complete museum provenance, visual design, eligibility rules, and precise move interaction. Its public display in the City 2 museum and later theft by Team Abyss are now confirmed future canon. A special strike that severs a restorative veil or persistent supernatural connection is an earlier proposal. It is not implemented or fixed.

The ordinary battle move and the story-specific interaction must agree. A generic source of the same move must not accidentally unlock the unique narrative result unless deliberately approved. The resolution depends on this Suicune's recovery and the sabre's intended use, not merely on a menu displaying a move name.

### 7.6 Legendary and final demons

**Confirmed.** A Water/Ghost legendary's magic is connected to Lapras. Final confrontations involve water-demon boss Pokémon. The intended Suicune solution concerns a final boss.

**Open.** Legendary species, name, personality, history, form, availability for capture, relation to the sea, and whether it is among the final opponents. The number and order of final bosses remain open. Preserve room for the sea to exceed any one encounter.

### 7.7 Koga's Pokémon as characters

**Confirmed requirement.** The player knows real Koga's team well; pseudo-Koga's boss team shares none of those familiar team members. Do not explain the difference as an ordinary competitive team update.

**Proposed rule.** Once selected, give real Koga's team distinctive species and recognisable individual behaviours. Use an entirely different species roster for the clone to make the clue legible. Preserve the original team in the character record even if levels and moves develop.

### 7.8 Ordinary danger

**Confirmed.** Wild Pokémon can genuinely threaten communities. The regional ecology must permit frightening, hostile encounters without treating every dangerous animal as possessed.

**Proposed encounter variety.** Territorial danger, hunger, injury, displacement, criminal exploitation, and supernatural corruption create different problems. Some can be redirected or helped; some require fighting. A late demon should feel different from an ordinary creature guarding its young.

### 7.9 Tidebound Ekans and Arbok

**Confirmed, implemented in the current source.** Both local forms are Normal/Dark. Their original pixel silhouettes, shading and distinctive markings remain; the purple scales become neutral gray. Front, rear and party sprites match. Poison-type level-up, tutor and egg moves are replaced with Dark-type moves. This includes Coil, replaced by Hone Claws. Stats, abilities and the level-22 evolution remain unchanged.

**Implementation scope.** Newly encountered Ekans on the South Coast Road use the regional form and evolve into regional Arbok. Already-owned ordinary snakes retain their form and moves. No new Arbok encounter is added. Regional shiny artwork temporarily shares the gray palette. Their evolutionary or mythological origin remains open; no new lore is implied by this palette change.

## 8. Factions, danger, and everyday life

### 8.1 The criminal organisation

**Confirmed.** It loots and preys on poor, deserted communities. Its desperation and stupidity unleash water demons. Its actions interweave with the Koga story and ancient mystery.

**Working name: Team Abyss.**

**Open.** Final name, emblem, leadership, recruitment details, broader geography, and final fate. Do not create a conventional eight-city criminal campaign merely to resemble another Pokémon game.

**Proposed operations.** Control of boats, lamp fuel, valuable salvage, and access to supplies gives the team visible local power. Theft from shrines links material greed to old protections. A lack of conventional poison cures does not prevent conflict over food, shelter, clean water, or other supplies.

The organisation can be socially organised and still catastrophically ignorant. Its members need not have a coherent plan to summon an apocalypse. A decision to force one more passage or remove one more valuable object can have consequences they neither wanted nor understood.

**Confirmed gradual introduction.** The necklace quest shows petty theft, resale, packing, local jobs and foolish hierarchy before supernatural consequences. The south-coast storehouse routes coastal goods toward buyers at the docks. Its criminals lack a grand speech or coherent apocalypse plan. Later, the same infrastructure gives the organisation access to more consequential objects, including the museum's sabre. Recognition of potential value or power does not equal understanding.

### 8.2 The dojo community

**Confirmed.** Honourable warriors, a culture of practice, and Koga's useful discovery distinguish the mountain town. The settlement is capable of warmth and ambition before its corruption.

**Proposed internal tension.** Some residents value training as responsibility; others increasingly want certainty from one prodigy. The latter dependence makes his absence politically and emotionally destabilising. This tension must not imply that every apprentice embraces the clone's actions.

### 8.3 The old masters

**Confirmed.** They can entrust powers to a worthy challenger. Most later masters appear through incense only for the fight. Their empty shrines bear the trace of a former social order.

**Open.** Whether they shared an institution, how old they are, why each shrine is empty, and what each knows about the ancient wrong. Their collective history should not become an exposition shortcut that solves every mystery at once.

### 8.4 Ordinary life under pressure

**Design interpretation.** Poverty should be specific: missing labour, threatened transport, damp houses, an unmaintained path, or reliance on one defender. Residents still work, joke, disagree, train, care for Pokémon, and make choices. Suffering alone cannot establish a community worth protecting.

Show the cost of predation through what it prevents people from doing. Show successful help through what people resume. This makes the protagonist's eventual decision to side with humans an extension of lived relationships.

## 9. Full story outline

**Proposed chapter order.** The events marked confirmed elsewhere remain binding. These chapter divisions, transitions, and timing choices organise them into a coherent journey; they are not all separate approved maps or completed quests.

### Chapter I: The keeper's child

Before the player wakes, a sleeping Natu appears against darkness. It opens one eye. The words are: "A bird wants to play." The player guides Natu through a small, dim spirit maze towards the protagonist asleep in bed. Reaching the bedside dissolves this scene. The player then wakes in an apparently ordinary Gen 3 bedroom. Its exit returns them to the centre; its furnishings speak in impossible domestic fragments. Solving three consecutive bed questions and choosing to sleep for twelve years brings Wick into the room. Speaking to him leads into a larger, colder, fractured bedroom. Reaching its bed and answering four stranger questions opens the way into the established Natu hide-and-seek maze. A wrong answer in the second bedroom resets the whole attempt and returns the player to that room's entrance. The room forms a classic Pokémon maze with directional push tiles and teleporters. Reaching Natu ends the game: Mother calls from downstairs, and the player returns to the ordinary small bedroom. The maze appears only at the start of a new journey. Much later, the story explains that it was Natu's psychic projection; the opening does not disclose its cause. In the main hall, Makuhita moves boxes; Mother's affectionate scolding establishes their household life. No travelling companion has yet been chosen. Mother speaks of being old enough to explore and of the things she wants to teach while she still has time. The other village residents are elderly. Occasional remarks about the protagonist's unchanged youth initially pass as familiar affection.

Mother sends the child on a hundred-step walk with Pookie, who sleeps beside the lighthouse. Near the pier, an optional incident lets the dog sense something invisible and run away; the player brings her back by speaking to her. After they return home together, Mother permits a choice of Natu, Makuhita or Poochyena. She then asks for oil. The locked-out seller needs his keys recovered from the northern wood before he opens the shop and gives the bottle inside. The lamp lesson follows. Avoid mandatory combat before companion selection or on the keys route.

A homemade pie rewards the oil errand. Mother recognises it and shares it with the child; a household Pokémon hopes for a crumb. Returning its borrowed plate reveals a robbery. Helping the seller recover a necklace naturally carries the child south beyond the familiar opening area. The first opponent lies badly, loses and runs; a second leads to an ordinary criminal storehouse. Recovery first reveals the seller's quiet grief for his wife. A small pearl glint and an incidental vault remark await later reinterpretation, without changing the meal's sincere warmth.

Later lore reinterprets this permission: Mother's ritual arrested the child's ageing, her guilt sustained overprotection, and her impending loss of the ability to care now forces release. The opening does not state those truths. The sea dominates the surroundings before anyone explains it.

Pookie's optional pier reaction first suggests an unseen presence. A later pier visit can reveal Lapras as a ghostly horror; its identity and role remain unclear. Early encounters reveal both dangerous wild Pokémon and the human pressure on a poor settlement. Leaving home follows an obligation or local need rather than a professor's instruction.

**Emotional purpose:** establish a home worth returning to and a world that does not revolve around welcoming a new trainer.

### Chapter II: The paths between people

The great forest introduces a larger region, Natu's ordinary presence, riddles, and limited resting places. The player helps people in small ways. One success and one failure can establish that outcomes differ.

An early death or story-supported encounter introduces astral traversal and companion recovery. The first account of the protagonist's unusual survival treats it as luck. Proposed: if the player never loses an optional battle, an alternate scene reveals the affinity without forcing the death of their Pokémon.

The player visits the cherished refuge while it remains beautiful. Early shrine activity introduces worthiness and incense. The exact first master and power remain open.

**Emotional purpose:** make the player vulnerable while showing why continuing to help has meaning.

### Chapter III: Warmth in the snow

The player reaches the winter settlement. Koga is alive, lively, ambitious, and useful. His sister and the apprentices give him a place in a community. Stories about his mother establish a history beyond the protagonist's arrival.

Several encounters establish Koga's team and his discovery about poison defence. The player also visits the second settlement and sees why people depend on him. The organisation's exploitation links these journeys to the wider crisis.

Lapras begins moving from apparition toward trusted companion during this broad middle opening. The precise sequence may overlap with the mountain chapters.

**Emotional purpose:** allow a sustained period of affection and competence. Koga must not disappear immediately after his introduction.

### Chapter IV: The missing protector

Koga disappears. His sister, the second village, forest routes, and people he helped offer leads. The player undertakes a significant search. It should include real discoveries and practical help, even though it will not reveal his fate.

Rumours suggest a return. Other rumours mention poison conjuring and something going wrong. Earlier warnings about poisoning the mind become tempting explanations. Do not authenticate any of these as an explanation of real Koga's disappearance.

As danger grows, the player understands why the community wants a defender back. The criminals' actions increase pressure without supplying a convenient answer to the mystery.

**Emotional purpose:** turn absence into a condition of travel, rather than a brief suspense beat before a guaranteed rescue.

### Chapter V: The returned friend

The player finds the figure they believe is Koga. For a time, the reunion feels almost perfect. Both seem altered by magic, and the protagonist feels more understood than before. The player's own condition can be partially recognised here without revealing Mother's full role yet.

They share an astral-plane quest. Proposed: an escort or path-finding task lets them cooperate and talk in the fog, with a complete local objective. It must not require permanent loss of an established companion just to unlock the scene.

Something then breaks the familiar pattern. The returned figure acts off character, badly, or brutally. Escalation follows gradually enough for affection, doubt, and rationalisation to coexist.

**Emotional purpose:** make the later revelation destroy something the player experienced as real companionship.

### Chapter VI: The poisoned sanctuary

The winter settlement's defensive poison expands. Snow becomes stained, sludge collects, and the protection's costs spread. The player learns that the cherished forest refuge has been badly poisoned and goes to help before the final confrontation with pseudo-Koga.

Poison Pokémon fill the changed area. The dying guardian identifies the corruption of Suicune. The player confronts him and chooses between the lethal route and capture. Capturing him begins healing, whose completion may extend into later chapters.

Proposed: evidence in the watershed connects the refuge's harm to the settlement's defence. Criminal pressure remains part of the circumstances but does not remove pseudo-Koga's responsibility for the poisoning.

**Emotional purpose:** make a supposedly protective act destroy something already loved. Establish the consequential Suicune branch before the finale advertises its reward.

### Chapter VII: The shape of protection

The player confronts pseudo-Koga in the corrupted winter settlement. His insistence on protection has become destructive control. He deteriorates into a disfigured, cloudlike poison horror.

The boss team is entirely different from the real friend's familiar team. The absence is a crucial clue. The player defeats and destroys the false figure.

Later evidence, including the villagers' role, establishes that the returned protector was a conjured imitation. There was no real Koga inside it. The search for the actual friend remains unresolved, as it was before the reunion.

Some local harm can be reduced. The sister and surviving communities can continue. Those outcomes do not replace the missing person with a satisfying answer.

**Emotional purpose:** grief for a false return, horror at the destruction, and renewed solitude.

### Chapter VIII: Beneath the familiar coast

The continuing criminal crisis and the ancient mystery draw the player toward Dive and submerged routes. The remains of former habitation establish what the demons once were and what human greed did.

Lapras's unsettling connection deepens after a genuine bond has formed. The legendary account arrives near the end of this larger discovery sequence. It explains the magical link while leaving the sea's deepest nature unresolved.

Mother's role in the protagonist's curse is revealed late, including her belief, her partly self-directed or religious motive, the arrest of childhood, and the guilt behind her overprotection. Her precise objective and confession remain open. The protagonist recognises why the demons cling to them and why they can face the final threat.

If Suicune was captured, the healing and sabre quests gain their late relevance. The no-Suicune route continues toward the same necessary confrontation.

**Emotional purpose:** establish a persuasive case for the demons' grievance and complicate the protagonist's identity.

### Chapter IX: The last shrine

Very late in the journey, the magical cemetery presents the player's fallen companions. The final master fights with those Pokémon when enough exist, using his own team where necessary under the eventual selection rule.

The trial joins the stories of the villagers, pseudo-Koga, Mother, and the player. Each has confronted loss, protection, and the temptation to go too far. The player must face their own capacity for corruption without being told that every failure was a sin.

The encounter permits recognition or farewell. It does not automatically resurrect the permanently lost or produce the real Koga.

**Emotional purpose:** move from trying to undo every absence toward deciding how to act with a truthful understanding of oneself.

### Chapter X: The people on the shore

The protagonist chooses to protect people. The demons' historical grievance remains valid, but innocent living people suffer and human kinship carries decisive weight.

Final water-demon confrontations remain winnable through ordinary capable play. A healed Suicune with the mystic sabre supplies the intended special resolution through Behemoth Blade. Without that solution, the player can win the fight yet leave something irreparable behind.

The ending shows concrete effects in places and relationships the player knows. Hope does not demand a complete explanation of the sea, a return of Koga, or the restoration of every dead companion.

**Emotional purpose:** a choice for continued care under incomplete knowledge and permanent loss.

## 10. Major quest structures

### 10.1 Lapras: four meanings of one companion

**Confirmed sequence:** frightening ghost, beloved presence, deeply unsettling revelation, late legendary explanation. Each stage must preserve what the preceding stage achieved.

**Proposed progression.** Recognition precedes trust; trust precedes reliance; reliance precedes the disturbing fact. Give the player at least one quiet, unnecessary interaction that expresses affection. Do not make every appearance an efficient ferry service or a lore delivery.

**Open decisions:** recruitment, capture rules, availability for battle, relation to Surf or Dive, exact unsettling truth, and final fate. Lapras must not become a mandatory invulnerable party slot merely because it has narrative importance.

### 10.2 Koga: a search with a false answer

**Confirmed structure.** Know the real friend and team; experience his disappearance; search across at least two towns; hear return and conjuration rumours; believe in the reunion; share astral companionship; witness cruelty; confront forest harm before the clone boss; destroy the imitation; learn its origin; remain unable to explain real Koga's fate.

**Proposed clues.** An altered response to an apprentice, an unfamiliar approach to a familiar Pokémon, or a protective action with a disproportionate cost. Use a few consistent discrepancies. Do not hide every clue until a final monologue.

**Ending obligation.** The quest can resolve the conjuration and help surviving residents. Its original goal, finding the real Koga, remains unfulfilled. Do not label the entire social recovery meaningless because the search has no answer.

### 10.3 The poisoned forest and Suicune

**Confirmed structure.** Establish the refuge, reveal contamination, meet the dying guardian, confront corrupted Suicune, permit killing or capture, and connect the damage to the wider Koga crisis. The confrontation precedes the pseudo-Koga boss.

**Proposed choice handling.** Distinguish battle defeat from deliberate killing. An explicit decision after subduing Suicune can prevent a normal attack from silently selecting an irreversible ending. The exact interface remains open. Make permanence clear enough to understand without announcing every late-game consequence.

**Proposed healing structure.** A short sequence of care, a meaningful place, and a resource obtained through helping or exploration. Avoid a long generic ingredient grind. Restored Suicune should participate in the relationship, rather than functioning only as a purchased ending key.

### 10.4 The criminal breach

**Confirmed goal.** Connect local predation to the act that unleashes the water demons and the late ancient mystery.

**Proposed structure.** A small early intervention reveals how the organisation controls a route or resource. Later evidence connects those operations to looting a protected place. A final attempt to force access causes or worsens the breach. The player deals with its consequences after defeating particular criminals.

**Open.** Whether the player witnesses the breach, arrives too late, or participates unknowingly. Do not blame the player for an unavoidable scripted act without giving the story a reason beyond manufactured guilt.

### 10.5 Mother and the lower lamp

**Confirmed core:** the lighthouse home, Mother's belief in the water mythology, her ritual for reasons beyond the child's welfare, arrested childhood, guilty overprotection, eventual release prompted by her age, and the late curse revelation. **Proposed link:** the downward lamp offers recurring evidence of her keeping practices.

The quest should move from ordinary routine to discrepancies, then specific questions, then an account of the protagonist's condition. Her precise religious aim within the established mixed motive and the lamp's function remain authorial decisions. The confession should not become a universal solution to every open mystery.

### 10.6 The sabre

**Confirmed purpose:** enable healed Suicune's Behemoth Blade and the intended final resolution. **Proposed timing:** make the sabre's significance legible after the player has already chosen whether to save Suicune.

**Confirmed future canon, not implemented.** The same unique sabre is publicly displayed in City 2's important museum. Later Team Abyss steals it, recognising unusual significance without understanding its function, Suicune's role or the final confrontation. This theft grows from their established dock-city access, fencing and distribution infrastructure. Its complete provenance and the circumstances of recovery remain open. Earlier suggestions involving shrines or submerged history may inform that provenance or recovery; they no longer leave its initial display location undecided.

Much later, recovery of or access to the sabre connects to healed Water/Grass Suicune, Behemoth Blade and the intended better resolution. Merely knowing the sabre exists must not expose this purpose early. No museum map, heist script, sabre item, Suicune interaction or ending implementation belongs to the necklace milestone.

### 10.7 The oil seller's plate and necklace

**Confirmed immediate sequence, implemented in 0.6.0.**

1. Retrieve the shop keys in the northern wood, return them, enter the shop and receive lamp oil. The seller also offers homemade pie on a distinctive ceramic plate and asks for the plate back. City 4 enters casually through the recipe.
2. Return the oil to Mother and share the pie. She recognises the cooking without explaining how. A remaining household Pokémon moves closer to the food. She washes the empty plate. The player retains it for return. The lamp lesson remains available in its existing order.
3. Returning the plate to the shop triggers two boys bursting out and fleeing south. The seller's short, unsuccessful chase exposes his aching legs. He notices the plate and thanks the child before asking, neighbour to neighbour, for help recovering a pearl necklace.
4. Follow the south coast road. Optional wild Pokémon precede the first thief. He recognises the lighthouse child, lies badly and battles. After defeat he accidentally admits the other boy has the necklace, then runs.
5. Farther south, the second boy enters an old storehouse. The player overhears several criminals discussing resale, boxes, pearls, petty hierarchy and buyers at the docks. The runner and second thief can be defeated; the player recovers the necklace.
6. Return it to the seller. Only now does he explain that his wife wore it on important and ordinary days. His grief is restrained. A single pearl briefly catches unusual light; he explains it as something pearls do.
7. Wondering where to keep it, he mentions the museum down at the docks, dismisses it as too important for this personal object, then casually suggests Ellie's huge lighthouse vault. He puts the necklace away. No vault purpose or museum quest is revealed.

**The pearls: confirmed future meaning.** The necklace is not a unique legendary artefact. It uses a particular kind of unusual pearl belonging to the deeper magical ecology, and many more exist. Neither seller nor protagonist understands this yet. Its emotional value comes from its wearer, not hidden power. Later recognition reveals that the deep magical world had long existed within ordinary human life. Do not call these pearls sacred, cursed, demonic, chosen or legendary in early content. Their specific ecology and name remain open.

**Narrative method.** Ordinary task → relationship → small reward → domestic warmth → new obligation → travel → mundane criminal conflict → personal meaning → delayed reinterpretation. Let the pie be pleasant and the thieves fun. Avoid turning every sentence into a clue or every form of mourning into corruption.

**Long-range structure, recorded without later implementation.** Early: seller → necklace → petty thieves → small Abyss operation → road toward City 2. ## 11. Shrines, trials, and traversal

### 11.1 Structure

**Confirmed.** Old masters entrust powers after the player proves worthy. Most later shrines are empty. A special incense must first be found, then brought to summon a master only for the fight. There is no normal gym circuit.

**Open.** Number of shrines, individual masters, associated powers, exact incense varieties, and whether early masters are alive or appear by another method. The winter dojo's living trainer culture can coexist with mostly empty later shrines.

### 11.2 Proposed trial grammar

A local need or an obscure trace leads to incense. Reaching the shrine requires exploration or a riddle. The battle expresses the master's discipline. Victory grants permission, knowledge, or a field power. The community's response makes the outcome matter beyond a collectible emblem.

Possible trial themes include attention, restraint, endurance, keeping a promise, and recognising oneself. Only the final shrine's self-confrontation theme is confirmed. Avoid assigning one moral lesson to every type before the world and mechanics support it.

**Proposed retry rule.** Once a player has earned access, a failed battle should not automatically require farming another rare incense. Whether incense is consumed, renewable, or treated as a permanent qualification remains open.

### 11.3 Dive

**Confirmed.** Dive must exist as an HM or equivalent field ability. Underwater exploration is structurally important, not optional decoration.

**Open.** Who grants it, what shrine proves worthiness, which Pokémon can use it, and whether using it occupies a conventional move slot. Avoid making progression depend exclusively on an optional Pokémon that can be permanently lost. A compatible fallback route must preserve finishability without duplicating Suicune's unique ending role.

Other traversal powers and their sequence remain open. Use abilities to change the meaning and accessibility of familiar places; do not automatically recreate the franchise's complete HM list.

## 12. Death, recovery, and the cemetery

### 12.1 Intended recovery economy

**Confirmed.** No ordinary poison-healing items, Full Heals, or conventional Pokémon Centre reset. Small resting fires associated with travelling ninjas or comparable figures provide limited recovery at low HP. Death can first restore the player in the astral plane, where fallen Pokémon must be sought again.

**Open.** Treatment of ordinary Potions and other HP resources, status-curing moves, abilities, status-curing berries, held items, PC recovery, poison persistence after death, and the final rules for individual fainting. These omissions must become deliberate balancing choices. An item list alone does not implement the intended economy.

### 12.2 Seeking a companion

**Confirmed emotional structure.** The player searches the fog for an individual they knew. The Pokémon roam, can be fought, and require recapture. Odds are not generous. A very limited opportunity can end in permanent disappearance.

**Prototype rule, not final balance.** A whole-party loss in an integrated living-world battle triggers the astral sequence. An individual faint during a won battle does not currently impose permanent loss. Each spirit has one encounter with a six-round deadline and an engine catch-rate parameter of 35. That parameter is not a 35% probability; several throws can occur within the encounter.

**Prototype support.** A borrowed Natu enables astral battles, supplies are granted once per journey, and a different fallback Natu permits continuation with no recovered companions. These are provisional solutions to playability, not settled lore. Avoid explaining every test accommodation as a cosmic law.

The recovered Pokémon must be the same individual in all meaningful respects: name, owner, form, moves, characteristics, held item, and history. A species replacement is not a reunion. Technical failures should not deliberately count as narrative failure.

### 12.3 Limited rest

**Confirmed revision, implemented in 0.6.1.** Each fire can heal the party once every 15 real minutes. The cooldown belongs to that particular fire and persists in saves; time away from the game counts. **Implementation choice:** eligible rests restore full HP and PP, including fainted party members, but leave poison and other status conditions unchanged. Eggs and borrowed astral guides are excluded. Astral recapture and return retain their separate 25% HP floor.

**Field resources, implemented in 0.6.1.** Five berry trees supply HP-restoring Oran or Sitrus Berries. Each gives two berries and regrows after one real hour; these quantities and regrowth timing are provisional balancing choices. Status-curing berries are not distributed. Tall grass supplements visible roaming Pokemon: Aipom, Weedle and regional Wurmple in the Listening Wood; Zigzagoon, Sunkern and Ekans on the South Coast Road. Ordinary paths remain free of random encounters. All these encounters use the existing astral-loss rules.

**Presentation refinement.** Small pale threshold stones mark map passage tiles. Selected windows and tended lanterns cast amber light while ordinary locations remain permanently night. This adds local warmth without changing the global night palette.

**Design requirement.** Scarcity must permit continued play. The route with lost companions and the no-Suicune ending must remain viable. Preserve the emotional pressure of recovery without accidentally making a mathematically impossible journey.

### 12.4 Permanence and saves

**Confirmed intent.** Failed recovery can have permanent consequences in the player's continuing story. **Open implementation.** The final save/reload policy is undecided. The prototype uses ordinary manual saves; loading an older save can reverse a loss. No ironman or anti-reload policy is approved.

Make the distinction between a normal battle faint, a death event, an unresolved spirit, and a permanent memorial understandable. Do not promise final rules that have not yet been designed.

### 12.5 The cemetery gallery and battle

**Confirmed.** A late magical cemetery contains a gallery of fallen Pokémon. If enough exist, the shrine leader uses exactly those Pokémon for his team. Otherwise he has an impressive team of his own.

**Open selection detail.** Whether a short memorial roster produces a mixed team or the master's full fallback team. The prototype helper fills vacancies with fallback Pokémon and selects up to six recent permanent losses. That is a provisional implementation, not a fixed interpretation of every possible roster. More than six losses also require an approved selection rule.

The archive should preserve individuals, not only species silhouettes. Recognisable names and histories are central. The encounter must not mutate or overwrite their memorial records.

**Design interpretation.** The gallery acknowledges absence. The battle asks the player to face their relation to loss and control. It should not imply that every lost Pokémon suffered because the player was morally defective. A player who lost nobody still deserves a complete, meaningful shrine encounter. No minimum death quota is intended.

The trial does not automatically bring the lost back. Its relation to the Koga story is acceptance of an unresolved absence and recognition of what grief can tempt a person to do.

## 13. Endings and irreversible consequences

### 13.1 The shared commitment

**Confirmed.** The protagonist ultimately sides with people because living innocents suffer and human kinship remains stronger, even after the demons' case becomes persuasive. A fully developed demons-aligned alternate campaign is not established.

Victory must not falsify the ancient account or declare the victims' grievance illegitimate. The protagonist's decision is morally situated. They can protect present people and still acknowledge the debt those communities inherited.

### 13.2 The more hopeful route

**Confirmed core.** The player saves and heals Suicune into Water/Grass, obtains the mystic sabre, and gains the intended final use of Behemoth Blade. The result permits a better resolution than ordinary victory without him.

**Proposed mechanism.** The special strike breaks a continuing supernatural connection that defeating the boss's immediate manifestation cannot break. This can explain why a mechanically winnable fight still has different consequences. The exact connection, cost, and scene remain open.

**Proposed aftermath.** Signs of living water return, some routes become usable, and familiar survivors resume small activities. Keep improvement concrete and partial. Koga can remain missing, companions can remain dead, and history can remain painful within a hopeful ending.

### 13.3 The darker victory

**Confirmed.** Without Suicune, the game remains finishable. Winning the final fight does not make everything right. Some remaining harm lies beyond the player's power to fix.

**Open.** Which places remain damaged, whether the sea threat is contained or merely altered, the protagonist's final condition, and the fate of specific characters. Avoid fixing these through a casual descriptive flourish.

**Proposed aftermath.** The immediate attack stops, but certain waters or homes remain inaccessible, a haunting persists, or a necessary separation cannot be ended. Choose the final form of loss once the boss mechanism is settled. It must feel like a consequential outcome of the same world, not an unrelated punishment scene.

Do not add a secret grind, replacement legendary, generic Behemoth Blade user, or late item that silently recovers the identical hopeful outcome after the unique solution is gone.

### 13.4 Edge cases to settle before implementation

The final story logic must address Suicune captured but unhealed, healed but later lost, the sabre found without him, possession of the item without its intended use, and a player who never reached the refuge. These conditions are not all approved independent endings.

**Proposed provisional rule.** The hopeful resolution requires the actual recovered Suicune and the sabre's intended final interaction. Missing prerequisites preserve the winnable darker route. Confirm the precise rule before encoding permanent ending flags.

Neither ending should automatically resolve real Koga's disappearance or the sea's ultimate nature. The hopeful route offers meaningful repair, not universal restoration.

## 14. Revelation and continuity rules

### 14.1 What the player learns, and when

**Proposed information order based on confirmed reveals.**

At home, the player knows Mother's ordinary role, their companion, and the local sea. Early astral survival receives a provisional explanation based on luck. Lapras is first experienced as frightening.

The pie and necklace sequence establishes Mother's familiarity with the recipe, the seller's wife, modest coastal crime, the dock-city museum and the existence of a lighthouse vault. It reveals no ritual, curse, lower-lamp function, vault purpose, deep-sea mythology or sabre plan.

In the mountain and river communities, the player learns who Koga is, knows his team, sees poison's defensive use, and hears the mental-corruption rumour. The absence comes after genuine attachment.

During the false return, the protagonist believes they share something with Koga. The astral quest strengthens that belief. Discrepancies and cruelty then challenge it. Poisoning and the unfamiliar boss team add evidence.

After the imitation's destruction, the villagers' ritual establishes its origin. This revelation answers "What did we meet?" while leaving "Where is Koga?" unanswered.

Late submerged discoveries establish the ancient victims and human wrongdoing. Mother's role explains the protagonist's condition. The legendary story explains Lapras's magical link near the end. Their exact local order remains adjustable, but none should be fully disclosed in the opening.

### 14.2 Mysteries with different functions

**Intentional unresolved mystery:** real Koga's fate. Preserve it even in optional content and the best ending.

**Intentional incompleteness:** the sea's deepest nature. Concrete historical evidence and particular supernatural rules can still be knowable.

**Reveal that must become clear:** the poison imitation is not real Koga; the protagonist was an existing person altered by Mother's magic; the demons' grievance arises from actual human actions; Lapras carries the legendary's magic.

**Authorial decisions still open:** Mother's exact religious objective, Koga's mother's absence, exact ancient atrocity, the guardian's identity, the legendary's relation to the sea, and the final boss mechanism. These are not automatically permanent mysteries. They need deliberate decisions about what writers know and what players learn.

### 14.3 Continuity safeguards

- Do not return the protagonist to the small garden house from earlier concept art. The lighthouse is home.
- Do not introduce a conventional rival or professor to explain menus or quests.
- Do not reveal the eternal-child ritual or Mother's guilt in the opening; use restrained village foreshadowing.
- Do not populate the starting village with other young permanent human residents without an explicit change to its established age pattern.

## 15. Art, sound, dialogue, and quest design

### 15.1 Visual direction

**Confirmed world rule: perpetual night.** Ordinary locations always take place at night. Retain the grey, dark, desaturated mood of the earlier opening; the brighter, cheerful autumn treatment in 0.5 is superseded. Autumn appears through worn foliage, stone, shore texture and small flowers under moonlight. Use charcoal, slate, cold grey-blue water and restrained traces of colour. Small lamps and sheltered interiors may offer warmth without turning the landscape into daylight.

Daylight or unusually bright surroundings are reserved for specifically approved magical locations later in the game. Do not add a normal day/night cycle, sunrise, or host-clock-dependent brightening. This is a firm world and presentation rule. Its narrative cause has not been established; do not invent one or reveal a supposed explanation in early dialogue. Ordinary battle scenes and time-of-day queries should also use night.

**Confirmed direction: familiarity before strangeness.** The opening should feel almost like a vanilla Generation 3 Pokémon reskin. Preserve its tile scale, familiar silhouettes, readable paths, movement and ordinary dialogue presentation. The coastal palette is dark and desaturated: slate rock, grey-blue sea, muted foliage, weathered timber, dim flowers and small amber lamp accents. Comfy autumn JRPG pixel art meets the sorrow and unease of Dark Souls and Twin Peaks. These are tonal references, not a request to copy their assets.

Introduce deeper mechanical changes, unusual scripts, animation and stronger visual departures progressively through the journey. Let the player first grow attached to a recognisable world. Early effects should serve a specific scene: a dog reacts to nothing visible; a quiet camera movement reveals more sea; a pale presence slowly becomes perceptible. Avoid front-loading a catalogue of custom systems or constantly advertising how modified the game is. Existing recovery mechanics remain in the prototype; this rule governs presentation and future pacing, rather than removing established mechanics.

**First coast, implemented in 0.5.** An irregular rocky lighthouse islet connects to the village by a worn stone causeway. Boulder clusters enclose the headland; a sandy beach sits in the sheltered bay. Muted autumn trees, flowers sheltered by stones, scattered shore rocks, sea glass and tended lamps provide signs of continuing care. The long timber pier faces an expanse of water; the map edge must stay outside every reachable view, including the encounter camera. Pookie's first bark reveals nothing. The later Lapras glimpse uses a pause, quieted music, gentle camera travel and a gradual appearance and disappearance.

**Later regional palettes remain proposals.** Forest greens and pale refuge light; clean winter whites before purple and green contamination; desaturated astral fog with recognisable companion colours. Keep terrain and interactive objects legible.

The town's transformation should alter surfaces, water, movement, and residents' routines. It should not consist only of a purple overlay. Beauty needs its own visual treatment: a clear interval after rain, a living garden, a companion at rest, or light moving through water.

**Asset status.** Existing art is temporary Essentials material. The current Lapras apparition uses a pale stock icon. Earlier concept images establish atmosphere, not a finished tileset or approved sprite sheet. Record final palettes and designs separately when chosen.

### 15.2 Sound and music

**Proposed direction.** The sea should have an audible presence even when off screen. Distinguish exposed coast, sheltered home, winter courtyard, forest interior, and astral shore through sound density and distance. Silence and changes in familiar sound can carry horror more effectively than constant stingers.

Musical material can return altered across related places: Koga's living warmth, the false reunion, and the polluted settlement; the refuge before and after contamination; the domestic lighthouse and the late return. Do not make every variation announce a twist before the scene does.

Use rare, sustained beauty rather than continuous melancholy wallpaper. A hopeful scene may permit a fuller phrase or harmonic resolution that the region usually withholds. These are compositional proposals, not an approved soundtrack specification.

**Prototype.** Two original ambient loops accompany parts of the opening. They are a starting point, not the final regional score.

### 15.3 Dialogue

**Confirmed.** Sad dialogue, riddles, solitude, and meaningful help matter more than frequent trainer spectacle. Koga and the apprentices must retain a notably warm register.

**Proposed writing rules.** Let characters know particular things rather than speak for the entire world. Mix practical conversation with implication. Use silence only when a scene gives it meaning. Not everyone should speak in cryptic prophecy.

Koga's warmth should be direct; Mother's evasions selective; the sister's stories personal; the guardian's grief attached to a particular place. Criminals can sound impatient, transactional, or frightened instead of delivering theatrical claims about world domination.

A riddle should connect to an observable feature, custom, or earlier statement. Obscure lore should enrich an answer, not make essential progression depend on guessing the writer's private symbolism.

### 15.4 Small quests and useful failures

**Proposed quest examples.** Repair a lamp for a route that only one person still uses. Carry a message to a house whose resident has already left. Clear a dangerous path and find that its intended traveller cannot make the journey. Help restore a shared room after poisoning. These are examples of scale and tone, not new confirmed events.

Each quest should define what the player understands, what they can change, what remains beyond them, and how the outcome appears in the world. Failure can still produce knowledge, safety for someone else, or a truthful farewell. Avoid writing every quest as an inevitable failure disguised by meaningless choices.

### 15.5 Horror boundaries for this project

**Design interpretation.** Terror should emerge from behaviour, attachment, scale, and the corruption of familiar places. Pseudo-Koga's brutal act and disfigurement need force, but the game does not yet have an approved graphic-violence level. Do not introduce gratuitous gore as a substitute for the intended sadness and mystery.

Maintain readable scenes, restrained flashing, and clear battle feedback. Mystery about the sea's intentions is productive; uncertainty about whether the game registered a button press is not.
